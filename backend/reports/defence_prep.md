# Defence preparation

**Part 1** brings in the examiner walk-through: everything that looked
wrong in the app, or could not be defended. **Part 2** gives anticipated
questions, with answers drawn from the evidence and the figure or table
that supports each.

**The answers are drafts in the team's voice.** You must be able to say
each one without notes, and change anything you cannot defend. Where an
answer has no evidence behind it, it is marked ⚠ and listed in
`reports/fyp1_outline.md` under "Evidence gaps".

## Part 2: anticipated questions

### "Where did this number come from?"

**Answer.** "Every number in the report is regenerated by one command,
`python -m scripts.make_figures`. It writes the figures and
`results/manifest.json` with the git commit, the model version, a hash of
the dataset's labels, the split dates and every seed. If a number is in
the report, it is in the manifest first. Live numbers carry the date they
were read, because the forward record keeps growing."

**Support:** `results/manifest.json`; `REPRODUCE.md`. One honest
exception: the model card's out-of-time 0.758 came from an uncommitted
run. The reproducible figure is 0.796, and we report that.

### "Why is accuracy not reported?"

**Answer.**
- "Because it is meaningless here. In the random cohort the true buyer-loss
  rate forward is 4.7%; at the 3 November floor it was 2.3%. A model that
  never flags anything would be 95–98% accurate."
- "We report PR-AUC with the base rate beside it, precision of the
  strongest warnings (P@10 0.90, P@25 0.92 in-period), and calibration.
  This was promised on slide 8."

**Support:** Figures 4 and 5; Figure 1 for the base rates;
`model_card_phase6.md`.

### "How do you know the clusters are real?"

**Answer.**
- "Two different senses of real. **Structurally:** wallet pairs inside one
  funding cluster trade the same token at the same time 3.6 times as often
  as random pairs when only buys count (p = 0.049), and 4.7 times for buys
  or sells (p = 1.3 × 10⁻⁵). A degree-preserving shuffle of the cluster
  graph collapses that to 0.26 times, so the effect is in the clusters,
  not the degree structure."
- "**But:** the counts are small, 3 of 40 pairs for buys only. The edges
  are token-acquisition provenance, not ETH funding, because no keyless
  source exposes native-ETH funding. And the strongest coordinated case we
  found is bundling: one contract, 81 wallets, two transactions (Figure
  10). Independent wallets buying together within 90 seconds: none
  found."
- "And the clusters do not help prediction (Figure 8)."

**Support:** Figures 9 and 10; `reports/cluster_validation.json`;
`graph/funding.py` docstring.

### "Why only this many tokens?"

**Answer.**
- "We proposed 5,000. We labelled 1,058, of which 983 were alive at minute
  5."
- "Labelling needs archive reads of each pool across 90 days, and the only
  keyless log endpoint caps a query at 1,000 blocks. The Phase 5 set alone
  was about 297,000 RPC calls."
- "So we used a case-control design: enrich the rare outcomes for power,
  keep a random cohort of 458 for the true rates, and correct the priors.
  Intervals are family bootstraps, so the uncertainty from the smaller set
  is shown, not hidden."
- "Separately, the live forward record has 10,233 resolved launches,
  judged before their outcomes existed, though on a 6-hour label rather
  than the 90-day one."

**Support:** Figure 1; `reports/sampling_design.md`; `phase5_counts.md`.

### "What stops the model from seeing the future?"

**Answer.** "Five things, and we found the hard way that the first design
did not have them."
1. "**The cutoff.** Features stop at launch + 5 minutes, at the block
   computed by the chain clock. Our first design froze inputs at 24 hours,
   and all 28 rugs happened inside that window. The features were
   measuring the outcome, so that result was withdrawn."
2. "**The split by date.** Train before 20 April 2026, test after. No
   operator family on both sides."
3. "**Out of time.** A model trained only before 1 March, tested on March
   to April: 0.796 against the checker's 0.331."
4. "**Forward.** The frozen model scores each new launch at minute 5, and
   the prediction is written to an append-only log before its outcome
   exists. Only predictions made before hour 6 count. The protocol was
   pre-registered on 19 October."
5. "**When the chain changed under us** (Base's block time on 5
   November), the exclusion rule was committed before the fix and before
   we looked at any new data."

**Support:** `reports/leakage_audit.md`; Figure 3; Figure 6;
`DECISIONS.md` Phase 8 and 2026-11-16.

### "Why is this a complex computing problem?"

**Answer** (⚠ **ours to frame**; the evidence that supports a framing):
- **No ground truth exists.** The label is defined by rule and tested for
  sensitivity: Figure 2.
- **The adversary adapts.** A scam wave on one launchpad template appeared
  on 3 November and moved the base rate fourfold: Figure 6b and
  `DECISIONS.md`.
- **Requirements conflict.** Catch rate against false alarms (Figure 4);
  calibration against ranking (Figure 5).
- **The data infrastructure is hostile.** Every free tier measured below
  its documentation (`honest_results.md` §5), and the chain itself changed
  its block time (§7).
- **Several fields at once.** Graph algorithms, machine learning, a live
  pipeline, and a typed contract between backend and frontend.

### "What happens when I type in a token you have never seen?"

**Answer.**
- "You get a page that says BlockLens has not researched it, and shows
  only live data: market data, holder count and honeypot flag, through our
  API, with plausibility checks on the market figures."
- "The model's panel says 'Outside the model's scope', and why. It judges a
  launch once, at its own minute 5, and that token was not seen launching.
  It gives no score, and never a sample one."
- "If you type a non-address, it says so. A Solana address: 'live lookup
  covers Base only'."

**Support:** a live demo. `e2e/fallback.spec.ts` ("a token the live feed
never saw is outside the model scope"); `docs/EXAMINER_WALK.md`.
